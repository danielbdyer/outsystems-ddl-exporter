## 2 The domain space (stated independently of the current code)

The plane's job: every change the engine makes between reading a catalog and emitting artifacts is a *transformation*, and each transformation has (a) an owner — the data itself, or an operator expressing a choice; (b) a family of outcomes it can produce (removed, renamed, tightened, retargeted, reordered, annotated, refused); (c) a position in a firing order with real preconditions (topology before graph analytics; logical naming before operator rename pins); (d) a witness obligation — every substantive firing leaves classified lineage, and every registered capability provably fires somewhere.

The operator-intent side of the domain is the set of *decision channels* the operator actually has. Today that set observably includes: which kinds appear (selection), what physical/presentation form they take (emission), what extra content enters (insertion), what invariants strengthen (tightening), how under-determined order resolves (ordering), **how cross-environment user identity is bridged (user matching), and which references reroute through bridge entities (bridge retargeting)** — the last two being the youngest channels, added by chapters 4.2 and the 2026-07 bridge arc. A faithful axis vocabulary enumerates every channel an operator can act through, at the grain the operator thinks in, because that vocabulary is what conflict detection, per-axis registry queries, the manifest's applied-transforms enumeration, and the policy combinator DSL all quantify over.

The composition side of the domain has more than two postures: the operator-free baseline; the *evidence-informed* baseline (Policy.empty with a real profile — the definition of DataIntent is literally parameterized over profile); any single axis or named subset of axes; and full production. Pass order is not a preference — it encodes data dependencies (a pass consuming a product another pass writes) and dominance rules (pins over defaults), and each such dependency is a fact the domain says should be either satisfiable-by-construction or refused by name, never silently defaulted. Unimplemented or conditionally-firing capabilities (a pass whose substantive execution happens only at a seam or on a sink-backed read) are still domain citizens and need named firing conditions.

### F1 — The axis vocabulary and the Policy vocabulary have forked; the fork is actively mislabeling the two youngest operator channels

**Evidence.** The founding identity: "Policy IS operator intent, reified... Policy axes and OverlayAxis values are the same thing, structurally" (DECISIONS.md:11111). Weakened once, deliberately, by the Ordering addition: "the structural equivalence weakens from `OverlayAxis = Policy DU axes exactly` to `OverlayAxis ⊃ Policy DU axes`" (Classification.fs:15-16; DECISIONS.md:11632; codified into the axiom at AXIOMS.md:1521-22 "= Policy DU axes + Ordering"). Since then Policy grew `UserMatching` (chapter 4.2; Policy.fs:696) and `BridgeRetarget` (Policy.fs:702) with no OverlayAxis counterpart — the stated superset relation is now false in both directions, and Policy.fs:672 still calls the six-field record "the five-axis policy aggregate."

The two passes consuming those axes classify as `OperatorIntent Selection`: UserFkReflowPass.fs:67 and BridgeRetargetPass.fs:33. But `Selection`'s own contract is "Which kinds appear in the catalog (filtering, masking, inactive-records disposition)" (Classification.fs:25-27) — neither pass affects which kinds appear; both reroute *identity resolution* (user FK values; FK constraint targets). The strain is admitted in place: UserFkReflowPass.fs:64-66 carries "(Refinement candidate at slice γ harvest analysis: Insertion may fit as well as Selection...)" — never resolved — and the overlay-exercise test's own prose reaches for a nonexistent axis: "per pre-scope IDENTITY axis" (RegisteredAllTransformsBidirectionalTests.fs:288-290).

**Consequences (this is not cosmetic).** (a) `ConflictDetector.unreachableTransforms` exempts every `OperatorIntent Selection` event from unreachable-transform detection because "the removal itself [is] not a conflict" (ConflictDetector.fs:57) — so userFkReflow/bridgeRetarget events on a Selection-removed kind are structurally invisible to the exact conflict class built to catch wasted operator intent; correctly-axed events would flag. (b) `axisOfCode` knows four prefixes (ConflictDetector.fs:67-72); `userFkReflow.*` / `bridgeRetarget.*` diagnostics can never participate in `AxisContradiction`. (c) `TransformRegistry.byOverlayAxis Selection` and the manifest's applied-transforms axis enumeration (`(SsKey × OverlayAxis option)`, Episode.fs:84, ManifestEmitter.fs:960-972) report identity-rerouting work as selection work to the operator. (d) The deferral discipline's own trigger has fired unnoticed: the collapse refactor was deferred "to whichever consumer first needs to consult both vocabularies at one call site" (DECISIONS.md:11633) — `PolicyExpr.eval`'s `Override` arm (PolicyExpr.fs:143-150) IS that call site, matching OverlayAxis cases against Policy fields; it can address only 4 of Policy's 6 axes (M1: `Override` on UserMatching or BridgeRetarget is inexpressible) and maps `Override(Ordering, _)` to `Policy.empty` as a silent no-op (M5 in a house where downgrades are never silent).

**Misalignment.** The axis DU is an implementation-era snapshot maintained as if it were the operator outcome space; the actual outcome space (Policy's channels) moved. Vocabulary split across two DUs whose documented relation is false (M4); the founding assumption "the axis set is stable modulo evidence-gated expansion" foreclosed noticing that Policy expansion IS the evidence (M2).

**Candidate reified primitive.** `OverlayAxis` gains the missing operator channel(s) — the pre-scope's own word: `Identity` (covering user-matching and bridge-retarget reflow; or two variants mirroring Policy 1:1) — plus an executable relation law: a property test pinning `every Policy axis has a designated OverlayAxis` (a total `axisOfPolicyAxis` map in Core, the same shape as `WriteSignoff.allModes`), so the next Policy axis cannot land without an axis ruling. `PolicyExpr.Override` becomes total over the new set or refuses by name.

**Outcome-fluency bought.** Conflict detection, by-axis queries, manifest enumeration, and the DSL all regain the real operator outcome space; the classification of the two youngest (and riskiest — identity-plane) interventions becomes true.

**Effort** M (new variant + ~6 exhaustive matches forced by FS0025 + reclassification of 2 passes + digest/manifest baselines move + DECISIONS amendment). **Risk of inaction**: every future operator channel repeats the shoehorn; conflict detection stays blind where operator risk is highest; A41's stated type is wrong.

### F2 — The skeleton pole is structurally impoverished: pass-grain exclusion of a site-grain fact, with silent empty-topology analytics

**Evidence.** `skeletonView` keeps only passes whose *every* site is DataIntent (TransformRegistry.fs:376-384; the strictest-site rule, :392-396). `topologicalOrder` is mixed (sortKahn DataIntent + selfLoopHandling OperatorIntent Ordering, TopologicalOrderPass.fs:677-680), so the skeleton excludes it — pinned deliberately (SkeletonPurityTests.fs:25-60, eleven passes, topologicalOrder absent). But the skeleton *keeps* the DataIntent analytics that consume its product: centrality, boundedContext, schemaComplexity, cascadeShockZones all read `state.TopologicalOrder`, which no skeleton pass ever writes, and the lifts silently substitute `TopologicalOrder.empty` (PassChainAdapter.fs:72-74, 96-98) — every skeleton analytics result is computed over zero edges. The full chain fixed precisely this bug class ("the prior `registered None` wiring computed every metric over zero edges" — PassChainAdapter.fs:85-87, RegisteredTransforms.fs:143-152); the skeleton reconstructs it by construction, and no test can see it (skeleton-purity asserts only zero OperatorIntent events; SkeletonPurityTests.fs:63-76). Doc drift: Pipeline.fs:913 still says "the four pure-DataIntent passes."

Second limb (M1): DataIntent's definition is parameterized over profile — "reachable from `Project(catalog, Policy.empty, profile)`" (Classification.fs:86-88) — but the implemented skeleton offers only the profile-empty point: `projectSkeleton` hardcodes `Profile.empty` (Pipeline.fs:917-918) and `skeletonChainSteps` is built from `allChainSteps = allChainStepsFor Policy.empty Profile.empty` (RegisteredTransforms.fs:223-238). The *evidence-informed operator-free baseline* — the definition's own general case, and the natural "what does the data alone say?" posture — has no run vocabulary (`runSkeletonWith profile` does not exist).

**Misalignment.** The vocabulary can classify at site grain but can only *execute* at pass grain, so one OperatorIntent site drags a pass's DataIntent capability out of the skeleton and turns downstream preconditions into silent defaults (M2 — the structure makes "skeleton = whatever survives pass-grain filtering" the only expressible baseline; M5 — the topology precondition is a partial function totalized by a degenerate default).

**Candidate reified primitive.** Two-part: (1) `ChainStep` gains `Requires : ChainProduct list` / `Produces : ChainProduct option` (a small closed DU: `TopologyProduct | ProfileEvidence | …`); any chain assembly (skeleton included) validates satisfiability and refuses by name — or names the cascade ("centrality excluded: requires TopologyProduct, producer excluded"). (2) `runSkeletonWith (profile: Profile)` — the definition's free variable surfaced; the skeleton with default-resolution ordering (its DataIntent sortKahn site at `TreatAsCycle`) becomes a nameable posture rather than a structural impossibility.

**Outcome-fluency bought.** `--skeleton-only` stops shipping silently-degenerate advisory analytics; the baseline family (operator-free × evidence level) becomes enumerable; chain assemblies fail loudly on unmet preconditions.

**Effort** M. **Risk of inaction**: the trustworthy-baseline mode quietly reports zero-edge centrality/complexity to any consumer; every future mixed-site pass silently strips more capability from the skeleton.

### F3 — Chain order and its preconditions are convention in one list, not typed facts

**Evidence.** The one list is canonical (good — see anti-findings), but its load-bearing order facts live only in the header comment: "2 default-on logical-name emission passes (BEFORE `TableRename` so operator pins dominate) → `TopologicalOrderPass` → 2 graph-analytics passes (after topology is populated)" (RegisteredTransforms.fs:110-118). `chainStepsSplitWithPins` locates the profile boundary by string: `List.findIndex (fun s -> s.Metadata.Name = "topologicalOrder")` (RegisteredTransforms.fs:189-193). Because the lifts default missing products (F2), an order violation compiles, passes registered⇔executed, and changes semantics silently.

**Candidate reified primitive.** The F2 `Requires`/`Produces` vocabulary makes order *derivable or checkable*: an assembly-time assertion that no step precedes its producer (S-effort assert-only form), or full topological derivation of the chain from dependencies (M). The split point becomes "split after the step producing `TopologyProduct`."

**Outcome-fluency bought.** Reorderings and insertions become either provably safe or loud; the two-phase acquisition-overlapped runner's correctness stops depending on a string.

**Effort** S (assert) / M (derive). **Risk of inaction**: low-frequency, high-subtlety — the failure mode is a green build with wrong pin dominance or empty-topology analytics.

### F4 — Production decisions in the `Label` escape hatch: the newest operator interventions are second-class lineage citizens

**Evidence.** `AnnotationDetail.Label`'s contract is self-contradictory: "Production pass drivers MUST use one of the typed variants above" AND it is "designated for... any future pass whose typed shape hasn't yet been earned" (Lineage.fs:143-150) — with no named trigger for when a shape is earned (against the house's Skip-with-trigger discipline). Both youngest operator-intent passes ride it in production: `Annotated (Label ("userFkReflow.matched-by-" + strategy))` (UserFkReflowPass.fs:69-71) and `Annotated (Label ("bridgeRetarget." + evidenceNarration))` — a rich typed `BridgeRetargetDecision` (readiness verdict + per-check block/warn facts) flattened to one string at the trail grain (BridgeRetargetPass.fs:45-47). Downstream, `EventProjection.decisionOf` returns `None` for `Label` (EventProjection.fs:66-71 + the match tail), so these decisions can never project to `transform.applied`/`transform.declined` — they fall to debug-level `transform.lineage`. The tightening axes' outcomes, by contrast, are fully typed (`NullabilityDecision … PhysicalClaimDecision`, Lineage.fs:123-142) and reach the applied/declined taxonomy.

**Misalignment.** M5 (the decision-annotation function is partial over the decision-making passes — total only for the 2026-05-era ones) + M6 (the outcome vocabulary exists upstream as types and is stringified into the trail). The operator-facing outcome space "what did the engine decide?" is structurally unenumerable for identity-plane interventions.

**Candidate reified primitive.** `UserMatchDecision of strategy: UserMatchLabel * outcome: …` and `BridgeRetargetTrailDecision of reference: … * decision: BridgeRetargetDecision` variants (payload types already exist), + `decisionOf` arms, + a named promotion trigger on `Label`'s docstring ("a production pass may use Label only with a DECISIONS deferral naming its typed-shape trigger").

**Outcome-fluency bought.** applied/declined egress, trail diffing, and the explain drill-down see the two riskiest intervention families; `Label` stops being an unbounded side door.

**Effort** S-M. **Risk of inaction**: audit consumers regress to substring parsing exactly where the cutover's identity risk concentrates.

### F5 — Classification is asserted per pass, not derived from provenance; the "whose default is it" rule has four inconsistent applications

**Evidence.** Four stances on operator-vs-data ownership of a supplied/default parameter, all resolved in prose: (1) NamingMorphism — config-taking `Catalog→Catalog`, classified DataIntent because "the morphism IS data" (NamingMorphism.fs:32-35,124-136), with the skeleton test doubling down: "an operator-supplied non-identity Morphism is captured in the closed-over config at factory time, not in the Sites classification" (SkeletonPurityTests.fs:26-33) — i.e., an operator-supplied morphism would run inside the skeleton stamped DataIntent. (2) VisibilityMask — the same shape, OperatorIntent Selection (VisibilityMask.fs:98). (3) LogicalTableEmission — ships Enabled by default yet "default-on IS the operator's intent" → OperatorIntent Emission (RegisteredTransforms.fs:46-50). (4) AdvisoryTuning — "Every value here is a **default operator opinion**, overridable" (AdvisoryTuning.fs:5-7) consumed by DataIntent-classified analytics passes. The type `Classification` is carried on every event (good), but its *value* is a module constant; nothing in the type system connects it to whether the config actually came from an operator.

**Misalignment.** M6 on the EPISTEMIC axis — how-the-config-is-known (source-derived vs operator-declared) is exactly what Classification is supposed to reify, and for config-taking passes it is convention; M7 — the same question answered four ways with no stated rule.

**Candidate reified primitive.** Minimal: a rule table + property test ("a config-taking pass whose config can carry operator opinion classifies OperatorIntent; DataIntent config-taking passes accept only source-derived values") pinning each factory. Fuller: a provenance wrapper on pass config (`Supplied<'T> = SourceDerived of 'T | OperatorDeclared of 'T`) letting `registered` derive the classification — making the skeleton-purity property sound against mis-wiring instead of sound-by-convention.

**Outcome-fluency bought.** The skeleton-purity guarantee stops resting on every future wiring's discipline; an operator-sourced morphism/tuning becomes either expressible or impossible, not silent.

**Effort** M. **Risk of inaction**: a leak of operator intent into the skeleton with DataIntent-stamped events — the one drift sub-mode (mislabeled at source) the A41 property tests cannot catch, because the label is the evidence they check.

### F6 — `registered ⇔ executed` is name-grain; substantive-firing conditions have no vocabulary

**Evidence.** The chain registers identity-configured instances: `SelectionSuppression.registered SelectionSuppression.identity` — "the Pipeline's applyModuleFilter seam executes it with the operator's real axes, so registered ⇔ executed holds for the one pass" (RegisteredTransforms.fs:122-131, comment); `PhysicalClaimPass.registered []` — "the sink-backed model read executes it with the journal-assembled adjudications" (:128-132). The biconditional's executed side is satisfied by the identity stand-in; the substantive execution site (`Pipeline.applyModuleFilter`, Pipeline.fs:1922; the sink read) and its firing condition are comment-carried. `TransformStatus = Active | NotImplementedInV2` (TransformRegistry.fs:83-85) cannot say "active, substantively fired at seam S under condition C." The dormant-mutation class this invites is already named in the code: `SelectionPolicy.filterCatalog` F12 — "DORMANT, unregistered... TRIGGER: the day a live path invokes this, it MUST register" (Policy.fs:738-751) — guarded by comment and audit sweeps, not structure. Registered⇔NEEDED — "an outcome the operator configured implies this pass must substantively fire in this run" — is likewise inexpressible; each such binding is proven ad hoc (A49's three-way law for S9) rather than stated once in the registry.

**Misalignment.** M1 (the outcome "registered, substantively fired where promised" is not a value anywhere) + M6 (the dual-execution-site geometry is a documented *posture*, DECISIONS.md:31087-31091, not a type).

**Candidate reified primitive.** `Firing : FiringSite` on metadata (`InChain | AtSeam of SeamName | OnSinkRead | Dormant of trigger: string`), letting the bidirectional tests iterate per-site ("every AtSeam entry's seam names it in its executed-names pin") and turning the F12 comment into a `Dormant` value the deferral index can enumerate.

**Outcome-fluency bought.** The totality proof gains a per-site leg; identity-default registrations stop being indistinguishable from substantive ones; dormant capabilities become registry-visible.

**Effort** S (vocabulary) / M (per-site test legs). **Risk of inaction**: a flow that bypasses a seam while its config is set — configured intent silently unexecuted, the precise inverse of the drift pillar 9 catches.
