### F1 — the synthesis-source vocabulary (the specimen, generalized)
**Evidence.** One concept — "the synthesized identity of a deployed sequence" — under three conventions:
`SsKey.synthesizedComposite "OS_SEQ" [schemaName; sequenceName]` (src/Projection.Adapters.Osm/OssysTranslation.fs:117, the named synthesis authority for the OS_ family, lines 66–128);
`SsKey.synthesized "OSSYS_SEQUENCE" (sprintf "%s.%s" r.Schema r.Name)` (src/Projection.Adapters.Osm/OssysRowsetReader.fs:1022 — same assembly, bypasses the sibling authority, and its own comment lines 1010–1015 claims it "mirrors ReadSide's reconstruction ... so both lanes agree");
`SsKey.synthesized "READSIDE_SEQUENCE" (sprintf "%s.%s" r.Schema r.Name)` (src/Projection.Adapters.Sql/ReadSide.fs:1391 — the lane it claims to mirror, with a different source string).
**Why it binds.** `Synthesized` equality is structural over (source, basisParts) — segmentation included (src/Projection.Core/Identity.fs:39–46). CatalogDiff matches sequences by SsKey (src/Projection.Core/CatalogDiff.fs:164–166), and rename-plausibility requires equal `synthesisSource` (Identity.fs:297–304). So `diff sink:<env> live:<conn>` (both legal `Ref` operands, Ref.fs:18–25) reports every sequence as add+remove, unbridgeable by design. tests/Projection.Tests/SinkDiffViewTests.fs:78–95 proves only the within-lane case and documents the cross-lane hazard in its comment. The stale Core comment (src/Projection.Core/Catalog.fs:2074–2080) asserting "disjoint prefixes `OS_SEQ_*` vs `OS_KIND_*`" knows only one lane. Beyond sequences: `READSIDE_IDX` (short) sits beside `READSIDE_TRIGGER`/`READSIDE_SEQUENCE` (long) within one lane (ReadSide.fs:1371,1304,1391); Pipeline mints `"OS_IDX_LOGICAL"` (Readiness.fs:102), lowercase `"migration"` (MigrationDependenciesBinding.fs:201), `"GOLDEN"` (SliceApplyRun.fs:57) — four case conventions, no registry.
**Misalignment.** The synthesis source IS an identity namespace, but it is spelled as free strings at 20+ sites in five assemblies. The house already knows the fix: `DerivationReason` was closed into a DU precisely so "a typo can no longer mint a silently-different identity" (Identity.fs:48–57), and `StaticRow.readsideIdentity` centralizes one mint "so ... realizations mint IDENTICAL identities" (Catalog.fs:224–236).
**Unification candidate.** A closed synthesis-source vocabulary in Core (the `DerivationReason` precedent) or, minimally, one named constant per convention with the sequence lanes converging on `OS_SEQ` + typed 2-segment basis (the chapter-3.6 slice-δ typed-segments discipline; the dotted single segment is the losing shape). CAUTION: SsKeys persist via `serialize` into `V2.SsKey` extended properties (Identity.fs:149–157) — convergence needs a reconciliation rule for already-stamped identities, not a bare rename.
**Outcome fluency bought.** "Diff any two operands of one estate" becomes sayable for sequences; a future lane (LiveOssysConnection is reserved, CatalogReader.fs:62–66) picks its prefixes from a vocabulary instead of minting a fourth style.
**Blast radius.** Mint sites: 8 (OssysTranslation) + 1 (OssysRowsetReader) + 6 (ReadSide) + 3 (Pipeline) + 2 (Core) + 1 (Twin). Grep `"OS_SEQ\|OSSYS_SEQUENCE\|READSIDE_SEQUENCE"`: 3 src + 12 test lines. Assemblies: Adapters.Osm, Adapters.Sql, Pipeline, Core, Twin.Core, tests.
**Effort.** M (S for the sequence pair alone; M with the persisted-identity reconciliation).

### F2 — `EvidenceCache` ×2

**Effort.** M.

## 4 Anti-findings (near-collisions that are correct distinctions)

1. **fingerprint vs digest** — looks like one concept (content hash) under two names; it is two concepts at two strengths: fingerprints are collision-TOLERANT movement gates (CHECKSUM_AGG family — TableFingerprint.fs:23, EvidenceFingerprint.fs:12, EstateEvidenceStore.fs:30) where a collision "costs a stale reuse in the vanishingly-rare case"; digests are collision-RESISTANT integrity values (ServerDigest.fs:8–15 explicitly rejects the checksum family "for integrity"; RowDigestFold.TableDigest, PhysicalSchema.fs:417). Two nouns for two failure-cost regimes — keep.
2. **`Notice` vs `DiagnosticEntry`** — not aliases; NoticeSink.fs:20–22 declares Notice as "the operator-relevant projection of a `DiagnosticEntry`". Stage-named projection, exactly what pillar 8 wants.
3. **`Episode` vs `CatalogSnapshot`** — Episode.fs:49–56 contrasts them in the doc: single-plane point vs multi-plane co-record. Genuine grain distinction, self-documenting.
4. **`Estate.Finding` (Pipeline) vs `EstateFindingKind` (Core)** — instance vs vocabulary, split so `Projection.Targets.*` (compiling below Pipeline) can key on the same identity (EstateFinding.fs:5–9 states the reason). Correct stage placement, not a leak.
5. **`TableId` (Core) vs `TableCoordinate` (Twin.Core)** — structurally similar, semantically two planes: physical realization with a Catalog axis (Coordinates.fs:218–229) vs logical name path in a world where "the ejected SSDT estate carries no SsKey bindings" (Twin.Core/Coordinate.fs:7–22). The different names ENCODE the plane; sharing `SchemaName`/`TableName` VOs keeps the kernel single.
6. **The Verdict swarm** — nine `*Verdict` DUs is not split vocabulary; it is one register (a judged outcome) at nine judgment surfaces, each prefixed and RQA-qualified. `Estate.Verdict` (Unified/Converging/Forked) vs `Readiness` (Ready/Paused/Blocked) keep deliberately disjoint vocabularies per DECISIONS.md:27397–27398.

8. **`testKey "TEST"`** — test fixtures do NOT reuse production synthesis prefixes; Fixtures.fs:68–71 mints `Synthesized("TEST", …)` "so the typed payload is honest about its provenance". The right side of F1's line.
9. **`LineageBuffer` / `PassChainAdapter` / `BatchSplitter`** — generic-looking suffixes that pass the PLAYBOOK structural test (the suffix names the algebraic role); PassChainAdapter is watch-listed only for grep-collision with the Adapters.* assemblies, not for renaming.

## Verdict (summary)

The lexicon is strong at the family level — the tightening grid, verdict/finding/report registers,
codec/emitter/run planes, and the fingerprint-vs-digest split are exemplary domain-first naming.
The debt is concentrated where one name crosses an assembly seam: two `EvidenceCache`s, two
`Environment`s, three "Sink" planes, a "Ledger" that names both the law and its violator — and, at
the identity core, an unregistered synthesis-source vocabulary that makes the SAME schema object
mint different SsKeys per ingestion lane (the sequence specimen is one instance of a
five-assembly pattern). F1 is the only finding with silent-wrong-answer potential (cross-lane
sequence diffs); the rest cost fluency, qualification noise, and wrong-file landings.
