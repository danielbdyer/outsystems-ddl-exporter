Only Looking

What the first two milestones built, why each part has the shape it has, and what the shape costs.

read at c1ce443 on claude/v3-architecture-design-sl95mx · 19 October 2026

In its first two milestones, v3 learned to look at an environment's database without being able to touch it. M0 and M1 are 8,857 lines of C# and 15,102 lines of tests that answer two questions, read-only, as the caller's own identity. Does an environment match the repository at a tag? What does a branch change, property by property, the deploy scripts and the refactorlog included? Tests hold M1's exits 2 to 8. Exit 1, the laptop check against Dev, waits on a person.

Everything after stands on these two. v2 knew how much rests on reading well, and wrote laws, analyzers, ledgers and a register of banned words. Still its proving script published to whatever server a config file named, its profiler streamed row values, and its law tests carried 38 skipped stubs beside 82 facts. Its disciplines were real, and most of them were kept by hand.

M0 and M1 move those disciplines into the material. Each idea below closes with an entry in the parity ledger's manner: where it lives, what it closes, which v2 lesson it answers, and what it costs.

io · tests

A hand that cannot close

Capabilities are types, the plan says (V3_MILESTONES.md §2.1). In io/SqlServer.cs the rule is two sealed classes over one base. EnvironmentDatabase, a database dbchange/environments.json names, has a private constructor and no Publish. Copy, a database LocalServer made and registered, has an internal constructor and alone carries Publish and Permissive. The Permissive profile's constructor is private and its Of internal, with Copy.Permissive the one caller. Strict is the pipeline's own profile as DacFx loads it, its target stripped and any password refused, and the type itself sets the flag that matters:

var options = DacProfile.Load(new MemoryStream(_profile, writable: false)).DeployOptions;
options.BlockOnPossibleDataLoss = this is Strict;

The code then proves its own shape. CapabilityTests builds a small project against the io assembly with seven forbidden uses, one to a line, and asserts the compiler's exact refusal on each:

("_ = named.Publish(dacpac, strict);", "CS1061"),
("_ = PublishProfile.Permissive.Of(strict);", "CS0117"),
("_ = DacFx.Publish(named, null!, strict);", "CS1503"),

Without those lines the same file must build, so the legal path is proven too. Two more tests scan the IL of every method io compiles for the tokens of Copy's constructor and of Permissive.Of, after a call, newobj or ldftn, and fail on any caller outside LocalServer and Copy. A type cannot see where a connection string points, so R15 stands beside it: LocalServer refuses a copy on any host an environment names, by spelling and by DNS, and fails closed when it cannot tell.

The simpler design is a guard that throws for a named target. A guard protects the paths someone remembered. A method that does not exist cannot be called from any path.

io

Questions that answer in integers

A named environment's rows may be real, so v3 asks them only questions whose answers are integers. SqlServer.Measure runs one AggregateQuery at a time, and only io can make one; the planted project above calls Of from outside and gets CS0117. Of checks a ScriptDom tree and keeps the text ScriptDom writes back from it. What runs is what was checked, with no comment or second batch to hide in.

The outermost select list may hold COUNT, COUNT_BIG, SUM(CASE WHEN … THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), MIN or MAX of a length, CASE WHEN EXISTS, or an integer literal. Beneath it sits the rule I admire most. A boundary is a length or a literal, never a value read from the data, and = or <> between two columns is the one exception, kept for joins and orphan checks. The comment beside it, ABS(x - y) + x - y = 0 is x <= y, reads like the notes of someone who tried to break it. One refusal gives its reason in its own text:

ConvertCall or CastCall => new Offence(x, "CONVERT or CAST, which quotes the value it fails on; TRY_CONVERT and TRY_CAST do not"),

A failed CAST writes the offending value into Msg 245. A synonym is refused, since it can stand for a table on a linked server that the query's text cannot show.

The simpler design is a deny-list over text. Comments and quoting fool it, and it says nothing about what a legal SELECT returns, which is where values leak.

Here the admiration meets its edge. The allowlist bounds one query. It admits literal boundaries, so a run of queries with moving literals over one keyed row could still narrow a value. What bounds the run is who may build queries, and today no one does: the builders arrive with WP 2.3. The closure is half built, and the built half is the hard one.

io · kernel

Read whole, keyed once

DacFx already knows what a schema is: every object a type with named properties and relationships, a package and a live database read into one model. The element reader takes DacFx at its word. ModelObjects visits every user-defined object, descends through each composing relationship, and reads every property the type declares that the platform carries, secrets excepted, with each relationship's targets and their own properties, written Relationship[position].Property. Values land in the kernel's closed Value. There is no reading code per type. What the reader knows of particular types fits on a screen: eleven serialized names in io/ModelTypes.cs that break DacFx's naming pattern, found by measuring 45 object kinds, the four constraint types that can sit on one column, and the secrets it will not read.

The care is in the keys. An unnamed default, check, unique or foreign key on one column is keyed under that column, so it travels with the column's rename. Unnamed siblings are numbered by what they reference and then by their values, never by SQL Server's generated DF__ names, so a package and the database it was published to key alike.

v2 grew a reader per question. It read a schema four ways through three lifts (V3_MILESTONES.md:178), drained rows in fifteen hand-written loops (V3_ARCHITECTURE.md:946), and its Twin's AddObjects build never saw a refactorlog rename or a deploy script (§1 fact 8). A list of the properties we care about is a list of drift nobody will see. A column carries 29 properties and 4 relationships (§1 fact 6). One read covers them, and a change no pattern knows will surface as unclassified and go to proof.

kernel

A kernel with no clock

The kernel is pure by the absence of a capability. kernel/BannedSymbols.txt bans nineteen symbols, each with its reason:

P:System.DateTime.UtcNow;the kernel has no clock: the caller passes the time
T:System.Threading.Tasks.Task;the kernel is synchronous: effects and their scheduling live in io/

BannedSymbolsTests plants one use of each in a copy of the kernel project and asserts RS0030 on every line. The kernel's one pragma disables CS8524 for a switch over a closed enum (kernel/Error.cs:105), so that CS8509, a member with no arm, stays an error.

Purity lets two files be exact. Fingerprint.Of leads every list with its count, every string with its length in UTF-16 code units, every value with a tag and every key with whether it has a parent, then hashes with SHA-256. A test names nine pairs a looser encoding would confuse:

[InlineData("tags: false and zero", 3)]
[InlineData("counts: a null property and an absent one", 4)]
[InlineData("counts: one relationship of two targets and two of one", 5)]

Change.Between runs in three phases, each a rule of SSDT's: where each key goes after the refactorlog's renames, parent first; which before key each after key continues; then created, dropped, renamed and altered, with a rename in letter case alone recorded and left uncounted.

The simpler design is a convention held by review. v2's core was pure as "Enforced by NoUnsafeTimeInCoreAnalyzer + audit," with one named exception (archive/v2/CLAUDE.md:187-188), and its lint markers drifted toward what its own guidance called performance-of-compliance (archive/v1/AGENTS.md:99). A rule with an allow-marker accumulates markers. A banned symbol has nothing to accumulate.

kernel · cli

Refusal as data

An Error cannot be made without a remedy. Its constructor throws on a blank message or remedy and on a code whose first word names no member of the closed ErrorCategory. Contract.ExitByCategory maps each member to an exit with no discard arm, so a category without an exit fails the build. The exit table can only grow:

# The frozen exit table: a code is added here in the change that adds it to cli/Contract.cs; no line is ever removed, renamed or renumbered.

Every envelope refuses an exit its outcome does not list, and a blockedBy anywhere but exit 3. Register.Errors drives every error code the packages construct and fails a remedy that is not one imperative sentence led by a verb. The texture shows in a denied login, whose one sentence DriftTests asserts:

env:uat refused this identity (Msg 18456, SQL Server's message withheld); a lead's prediction will appear on the pull request.

The denial becomes a route: someone who can read UAT will. The number is kept and the message withheld, since SQL Server's text about a connection can name the server or the login. An exception nobody expected becomes internal.unexpected, its message dropped once the command has read a named environment's connection.

The simpler design is exceptions and a top-level catch, and its default is a silent downgrade.

tests · io

A login watched from the server

v3 says its read-only principal sends no DML, no DDL and no EXEC, and it does not take its own word for it. ReadOnlyLoginTrace opens an Extended Events session on the reader's login with NO_EVENT_LOSS. The test runs check drift on a matching environment, drifts a column and runs it again, runs read --from env:dev, then parses everything the login sent with ScriptDom, reading sp_executesql through to its statement. Where the Windows runner's LocalDB cuts DacFx's catalog query off past 500,000 characters, the trace counts write keywords token by token.

The session watches the wire, DacFx's traffic included, and DacFx's catalog queries are not v3's to allowlist. The ruling of 2026-09-25 admitted one EXEC, DacFx's xp_instance_regread of the default file paths (DECISIONS.md:54). The code then removed the need for it. Since 6da60be a read extracts the database once and plans package to package, which connects to nothing, so the test admits no EXEC at all.

Package to package has a price, paid in plain sight. DacFx refuses a case-insensitive model against a case-sensitive database on a live plan (SQL72030) and plans it empty between packages, so v3 makes that check itself, as plan.collation. And a plan over two packages takes no cancellation token, so once begun it runs to its end.

tests · ci

Documents that fail

v2's documents knew they drifted. Its rebuilt CLAUDE.md made any restated count a defect because "the predecessor's table drifted against the code within weeks" (archive/v2/CLAUDE.md:264), and v3's architecture called v2 "a system that knows its documents are wrong and has responded by writing more documents about being wrong" (V3_ARCHITECTURE.md:607). M0 gives the documents tests.

LAWS.md is generated. ci/laws.sh writes a row for every Law, Value and Exit trait, then prints what nothing declares, 49 rows of VALUES.md and the exits a person or a CI job still runs (LAWS.md:172, :221). Laws fails unless the file regenerates byte for byte. NoSkips fails on any skip; v2's AxiomTests.fs carries 38, and v2's own survival list warns that soft-skipped Docker tests "are indistinguishable from passes at summary level" (archive/v2/CLAUDE.md:153). NoRestatedCounts reads each hand-written document for a number beside a counted thing; v2's proof lane still announces "the 41 SamplePr facts" and runs 47 (.github/workflows/ssdt-agent-proof-lane.yml:3).

ParityResolves holds the ledger between the versions. Each of V3_PARITY.md's 127 rows must name a v2 file that exists, down to its line, and each clause of its v3 cell must name a test, law, value or exit some test declares, or wait on a work package whose milestone is still open:

private static readonly Regex NotHeldYet = new(@"— not held yet: (?:WP (\d)\.(\d+)|the cutover tools)$", RegexOptions.CultureInvariant);

Completion is computed from the exit traits and from the lines of NEXT.md that name a person's command (tests/Budgets.Tests/Documents.cs:111-115). When a milestone closes, every row still waiting on it turns red, and so does a row that still says not held yet beside a test that now exists. Promises in this ledger expire.

the budget

Where the weight sits

io/ holds 5,203 of its 6,400 lines. Appendix F planned 1,850 for io across M0 and M1, so io ran at 2.8 times its plan through M1. What the plan still gives io, from the BlockOnPossibleDataLoss reader in M2 to Knowledge in M7, comes to 3,630 lines, and 1,197 remain. The cli stands in the same place at a smaller scale. The kernel has room.

Some of the weight is width. The budgets count physical lines; 717 of io's lines run past 140 characters, the longest to 302, and 1,128 are doc comments, which is where the measurements live. DECISIONS.md:55 kept io's and the cli's ceilings until a reflow to 140 characters is measured, and a reflow adds lines. Either a ceiling rises with a decision line, or the rest of io is far thinner per feature than M1's was.

There is a reading in which it will be. M1's io met each edge once: SqlClient's error numbers sorted into denied, unreachable, timed out and failed (io/SqlServer.cs:35-38, :138-142); a file: reference refused when git tracks it, when its group can read it, when it names a Windows data stream (io/SqlServer.cs:502-651). M2 to M7 should mostly call Query, Measure, Extract, Plan and Publish, which exist. Whether they do is the first thing M2 measures, and the budget test will say so in red if they do not.

What the next waves add

M2 waits on the operator's go, and M3's shape on the answer to §17 item 20 of V3_MILESTONES.md. M2 is where the allowlist meets its builders and its closure gets the other half. WP 3.1 is where v3 first meets v2's own output.

What M0 and M1 leave is a tool that can only look, and that knows, in tests, what it may say about what it sees. The care lives where few readers go: a constructor marked internal, a regular expression over a ledger cell, a remedy that has to begin with a verb. v2 wrote its disciplines down and kept them by hand. v3 writes them into the material, where being wrong makes a sound. The next waves ask whether the material can stay this careful at a third of the thickness.
